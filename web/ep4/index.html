The Kicked Rotor

A rod on an axle, kicked once per second. Without friction, chaos and order coexist forever; and when the rod is quantum, its chaos stops. Click in the pictures to launch orbits.

1Kicks become a map

Between kicks the rod turns at constant speed $p$; each kick changes the speed by $K\sin\theta$: $$p_{n+1} = p_n + K\sin\theta_n,\qquad \theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + p_{n+1}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2\pi).$$ The Jacobian determinant is exactly 1: areas are preserved (no friction), so there is no attractor. Every orbit keeps its own character: closed curves (KAM tori), chains of islands at rational rotations $P/q$, and chaotic seas (red: positive Lyapunov exponent).

2The last wall: Greene's criterion

A KAM curve with rotation number $\omega$ blocks all transport in $p$. The most robust is the golden one, $\omega = (\sqrt5-1)/2$, the number hardest to approximate by fractions. Greene's criterion: follow the periodic orbits $F_n/F_{n+1}$ that approach it; the curve exists while their residue $R = (2-\mathrm{tr}\,M)/4$ stays below $1/4$. 3Chaos as a random walk

Above $K_c$, the speed of a chaotic orbit wanders without bound. If successive angles were independent, $\langle (p_n - p_0)^2\rangle = \frac{K^2}{2}n$. 4Period doubling without friction: δ = 8.7210

The centre of the main island, $(\pi, 0)$, has $\mathrm{tr}\,M = 2 - K$ and loses stability at $K=4$, where it period-doubles. The generic model of this cascade is the area-preserving Hénon map $(x,y)\mapsto(1-ax^2+y,\,-x)$. Explore it (click to launch orbits) and compare the cascade with Part 1:

5The quantum rotor: chaos frozen

Quantum rotor: $p = \hbar m$. A kick multiplies $\psi(\theta)$ by $e^{-ik\cos\theta}$, free rotation multiplies $\psi_m$ by $e^{-i\hbar m^2/2}$; classical strength $K = k\hbar$. The simulation below runs both the wave (2048 momentum states, FFT) and 4000 classical rotors starting at $p=0$. Watch the quantum spread saturate: dynamical localization, with $|\psi_m|^2\propto e^{-|m|/\lambda}$ and $\lambda\approx D/\hbar^2$.

6The hidden crystal

Fishman, Grempel & Prange (1982): the quantum rotor is exactly a particle hopping on a lattice of sites $m$ with energies $T_m = \tan\big(\tfrac12(\omega - \hbar m^2/2)\big)$. For $\hbar/4\pi$ irrational they look random: a disordered crystal, where waves are trapped by interference (Anderson localization).
